Before you start

A note from Melissa

I think about my job a bit like a brain surgeon would. I'm on call, always. Probably too much, if I'm honest. But the people on the other end are navigating some of life's most stressful moments: looking for work, starting somewhere new, finding their footing once they're in. And I don't believe in leaving them guessing.

I know that weight from the inside. Rereading an email five times before you hit send. Wondering if one more follow-up makes you a pest, or if it's too late to ask a question in week three. So I am the person who takes the weight off. Candidate or colleague, know me and you've got a safety net.

At Gap Inc. that meant 1,500+ interviews a year across Global Tech and Corporate Functions. A scheduling system I built myself with AI. Dashboards that influenced leadership decisions, not just informed them. And a weekly digest where a teammate's daughter graduating college got the same spotlight as the hiring update, because why wouldn't it?

People over process. We're people, not numbers. Keep going and feel it for yourself.

Melissa

What I believe

Before day one

Candidate experience is everything.

The first email. The wait between rounds. The "we went another direction." Every one of those moments tells someone how much they matter. Hired or not, I want every candidate to walk away feeling valued.

Day one and after

The welcome decides who stays.

Retention starts on day one. How you're welcomed, and how you're treated once the shine wears off, matters more than any perk ever will.

Just because

Ask how they're doing. Mean it.

A real "how are you?" goes further than people think. Check in for no reason. Let someone vent, and make sure they walk away feeling heard and seen. It's the fastest way I know to close the gap between corporate America and actual humans.

Not just at the holiday party

Real life belongs at work.

Graduations. Birthdays. Someone's big trip. Celebrate your people, your people's people, and everything in between, right alongside the business, every week. That's where work and life actually meet.

All the way up

Leaders are people too.

The best leaders are visible, reachable, and a reminder that we're all human. Share what's coming before it lands. And when you hold a listening session, show people what changed because of it.

The future I believe in

AI for the busywork. People for each other.

I built my automations entirely with AI. They cut my turnaround time in half, but the real win was what they gave back: more time for the conversations, check-ins and small moments that make a person feel seen. That's the future I believe in. AI should take on the busywork so people can show up more fully for each other, not less.

Does this company respect me, my time, and its people?

Candidates ask it. Employees ask it too, just more quietly. The answer should be obvious.

How I do it

The habits behind the work.

Dependable

Your safety net.

Whatever falls through the cracks, I've already caught it, and so have my systems. On call, to a fault.

Adaptable

Change is a challenge. I like that.

Priorities move. I'd rather chase a moving target than guard the way we used to do things.

Growth mindset

Never finished.

Every system I build is more garden than monument. I'm continuously tending it, pruning what doesn't work and growing what does.

The mantra

People over process. Always.

Call it a mantra. For me it's the test every single decision has to pass. Before I build a tool, send an email, or design a dashboard, I ask one question: who does this help, and how will it make them feel? Process exists to serve people. The moment it asks people to serve it instead is the moment I go to work.
